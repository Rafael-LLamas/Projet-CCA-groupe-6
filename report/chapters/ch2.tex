\section{Operation}

\subsection{Addition}
\subsubsection{Introduction}
With the displacement generators, we can simplify the addition of 2 matrices and thus reduce its cost by concatenating the generators of the 2 matrices.
From the property 10.7 and 10.8, we can pass from $O(n^2)$ for the naive algorithm to $O( \alpha \times M(n) )$ for the version that we implement.
We need A=(T,U) and B=(G,H)
 \begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Generator Addition}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Function{gr\_mat\_mul\_generator}{$T,U,G,H$}

    \State $G_C \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_Concat\_Horizontal}{T,G}$
    \State $H_C \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_Concat\_Horizontal}{U,H}$
    \State \textbf{return} $G_C, H_C$
\EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

 \subsubsection{Exemple}
We takes A and B, two randoms matrices, C the result we need to getof A and B.Every matrices are in $Z/_{13}Z$.
\begin{equation}
    A =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        0     & 7     & 10 \\
        5     & 0     & 7 \\
        0     & 5     & 0  
    \end{pmatrix}, 
    B =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        9     & 12     & 4 \\
        0    & 9     & 12 \\
        3     & 0     & 9  
    \end{pmatrix}, 
    C =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        9     & 6     & 1 \\
        5     & 9     & 6 \\
        3     & 5     & 9  
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
If we takes displacement generator of A = (T,U) and B = (G,H)
\begin{equation}
    T =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        0     & 1     \\
        1     & 0    \\
        0     & 0      
    \end{pmatrix}, 
    U =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        5     & 0      \\
        0     & 7      \\
        0     & 10      
    \end{pmatrix},
    G =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        1    & 0      \\
        0     & 0      \\
        9     & 1  
    \end{pmatrix}, 
    H =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        9     & 0      \\
        12     & 9      \\
        4     & 3  
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
and we do the concatenation of T,G and U,H to obtain $RES_G$ and $RES_H$ (we have to do the concatenation in the order that I cited the elements).
\begin{equation}
    RES_G =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        0    & 1     & 1 & 0 \\
        1     & 0     & 0 & 0 \\
        0     & 0     & 9  & 1
    \end{pmatrix},
    RES_H =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        5     & 0     & 9 & 0 \\
        0    & 7     & 12 & 9 \\
        0     & 10     & 4  & 3
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
The matrix that we get D :
\begin{equation}
    D =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        9     & 6     & 1 \\
        5     & 9     & 6 \\
        3     & 5     & 9  
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}

We can see that the matrix D is equal to C so our way to add the 2 matrices is correct in this case.

\subsection{Multiplication}
\subsubsection{Introduction}
With displacement generators we also can simplify the multiplication of 2 matrices and thus reduce its cost by concatenating the generators of the 2 matrices.
- The displacement generators A=(T,U) and B=(G,H) 

- $W = Z \times A \times Z^t \times G$

- $V = B^t \times U$

- a (resp. b) a vector which is the last column of $ Z \times A $ (resp. $Z \times B^t$)

This allows us to pass from $O(n^3)$ for the naive algorithm to $O( \alpha^2 \times M(n) )$ for the version we implement.
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Generator Multiplication}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Function{gr\_mat\_mul\_generator}{$T,U,G,H$}

    \State $W_{Temp} \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_apply\_Zt}{G}$
    \State $W \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_mul\_vector}{T,U,W_{Temp}}$
    \State $W \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_apply\_Z}{W}$

    \State $V \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_mul\_vector}{H,G,U}$

    \State $a \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_mul\_vector}{T,U,LastCol}$
    \State $a \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_apply\_Z}{a}$

    \State $b \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_mul\_vector}{H,G,LastCol}$
    \State $b \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_apply\_Z}{b}$
    \State $b \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_neg}{b}$

    \State $G_C \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_concat\_horizontal}{T,W,a}$
    \State $H_C \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_concat\_horizontal}{V,H,b}$
    \State \textbf{return} $G_C, H_C$
\EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
We use the vector 
\begin{equation}
    LastCol =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        0 \\
        0 \\
        . \\
        . \\
        . \\
        0 \\
        1
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
in order to retrieve the last column of the matrix, this vector is composed of 0 except for the last element of the column which is fixed to 1.

\subsubsection{Multiplication Vecteur}
To perform the matrix multiplication via their displacement generators, we need a function that multiplies a vector with displacement generators to avoid recomposing the matrices.

First, we initialize our result to 0
\begin{verbatim}
    error = gr_mat_zero(Res, ctx);
\end{verbatim}

We part from the principle that a matrix can be represented by several polynomials.
Thus, we determine that each column of our vector X is a polynomial. Each row of our matrices also corresponds to a polynomial.
To obtain our result, we perform the following operations:

\begin{verbatim} 
  for (slong j = 0; j < X_cols; j++) {

    error = gr_poly_reverse(ph, ph, m, ctx);
    error = gr_poly_mul(p_tmp, ph, px, ctx);
    error = gr_poly_shift_right(p_tmp, p_tmp, m - 1, ctx);
    error = gr_poly_mul(p_tmp, pg, p_tmp, ctx);
    }
 \end{verbatim}

With: 
 \begin{itemize}
    \item X\_cols le nombre de colonne de notre vecteur 
    \item ph,pg et px les polynome de H,G et X
    \item p\_temp le produit intermédiaire de ph et px
 \end{itemize}
For finish, we put p\_temp in res.

    \end{pmatrix},

    \end{pmatrix},

    \begin{pmatrix}

        3     & 0      \\

We need to calculate W and V before concatenating the generators of A and B to obtain the displacement generator of D.
\begin{equation}
    W =
    \begin{pmatrix}

    \end{pmatrix},

    \begin{pmatrix}

\end{equation}
Puis faire la concatenation de T,W et $\alpha$ pour obtenir $RES_G$
et la concatenation de  V,H et $\beta$ pour obtenir $RES_H$ (il faut faire la concatenation dans l'ordre que j'ai cité les éléments).
\begin{equation}

    \begin{pmatrix}

    \end{pmatrix}

We can see that the matrix D is equal to C so our way to multiply the 2 matrices is correct in this case.
