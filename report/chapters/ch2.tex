\subsection{Addition}
\subsubsection{Algorithm}
With the displacement generators, we can simplify the addition of 2 matrices and thus reduce its cost by concatenating the generators of the 2 matrices.
From the property 10.7 and 10.8, we can pass from $O(n^2)$ for the naive algorithm to $O( \alpha \times n)$ with this algorithm.

 \begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Generator Addition}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Function{gr\_mat\_mul\_generator}{$T,U,G,H$}

    \State $G_C \gets [T | G]$
    \State $H_C \gets [U | H]$
    \State \textbf{return} $G_C, H_C$
\EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

 \subsubsection{Example}

        9     & 6     & 1 \\
        5     & 9     & 6 \\
        3     & 5     & 9  
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
If we takes displacement generator of A = (T,U) and B = (G,H)
\begin{equation}
    T =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        0     & 1     \\
        1     & 0    \\
        0     & 0      
    \end{pmatrix}, 
    U =
    \begin{pmatrix}

    \begin{pmatrix}
        9     & 0      \\
        12     & 9      \\
        4     & 3  
    \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
and we do the concatenation of T,G and U,H to obtain $RES_G$ and $RES_H$ (we have to do the concatenation in the order that I cited the elements).
\begin{equation}
    RES_G =
    \begin{pmatrix}
        0    & 1     & 1 & 0 \\

\subsection{Multiplication}
\subsubsection{Multiplication Vector}
Perform the matrix multiplication with their displacement generators needs a function that multiplies a vector with displacement generators to avoid recomposing the matrices.
\begin{equation} \label{eq:SigmaLUV}
    C = \sum_{k=1}^{\alpha} L(g_k) \, U(h_k)\,V
\end{equation}
To do that, we takes the formula for the reconstruction and we transform our displacement generators into polynomials and make every operation into them.
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Multiplication of displacement generators with a vector}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Function{gr\_mat\_mul\_vector}{$G, H, V$}
    \State $n \gets nbRows(G)$
    \State $m \gets nbRows(H)$
    \For{$j \gets 0$ \textbf{to} $nbCols(V)$}
        \State $P_v \gets \sum_{r=0}^{m-1} V[r, j]  x^r$ 
        \For{$k \gets 0$ \textbf{to} $nbCols(G)$}
            \State $Pg \gets \sum_{r=0}^{n-1} G[r, k]  x^r$ 
            \State $Ph \gets \sum_{r=0}^{m-1} H[r, k]  x^r$ 
            \State $P_{h\_rev} \gets \text{Reverse}(P_h, m)$ 
            \State $P_{tmp} \gets P_{h\_rev} P_x$ 
            \State $P_{tmp\_shift} \gets P_{tmp} \gg (m - 1)$ 
            \State $P_{res} \gets P_g P_{tmp\_shift}$ 
            \For{$i \gets 0$ \textbf{to} $\text{Length}(P_{res}) - 1$}
                \State $C[i, j] \gets C[i, j] + \text{Coefficient}(P_{res}, i)$
            \EndFor
        \EndFor
    \EndFor
    \State \textbf{return} $Res$
\EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
\subsubsection{Multiplication matrices}
With displacement generators we also can simplify the multiplication of 2 matrices and thus reduce its cost by concatenating the generators of the 2 matrices.
- The displacement generators A=(T,U) and B=(G,H) 

- $W = Z \times A \times Z^t \times G$

- $V = B^t \times U$

- a (resp. b) a vector which is the last column of $ Z \times A $ (resp. $Z \times B^t$)

This allows us to pass from $O(n^3)$ for the naive algorithm to $O( \alpha^2 \times M(n) )$ for the version we implement.
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Generator Multiplication}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Function{gr\_mat\_mul\_generator}{$T,U,G,H$}

    \State $W_{Temp} \gets Z^t \times G$
    \State $W \gets Z \times \Call{gr\_mat\_mul\_vector}{T,U,W_{Temp}}$
    \newline
    \State $V \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_mul\_vector}{H,G,U}$
    \newline
    \State $a \gets Z \times \Call{gr\_mat\_mul\_vector}{T,U,LastCol}$
    \State $b \gets -Z \times \Call{gr\_mat\_mul\_vector}{H,G,LastCol}$
    \newline
    \State $G_C \gets [T|W|a]$
    \State $H_C \gets [V|H|b]$
    \State \textbf{return} $G_C, H_C$
\EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
We use the vector $LastCol = (0,.........,0,1)^T$, with the numbers of rows of the matrix you to retrieve the last column. This vector is composed with 0 except for the last element which is fixed to 1.

For the multiplication with vector,if we compare matrices of 128,1024 and 4096,we get:
$T(1024)\div T(128) \approx 9.709$,
$T(4096)\div T(1024) \approx 3.329$.
For $O(\alpha M(n))$ we expected 9.14 and 4.4 respectively.The first ratio is close to the expected value, while the second shows more deviation.
This can be explained by the cost of copying values into polynomials, which is $O(\alpha n)$ and is not yet negligible compared to M(n).
We tried with bigger value and got the same problems but we can confirm that the complexity is $O(\alpha M(n))$ asymptotically.
A possible improvement would be to copy the values into polynomials more efficiently, which would reduce the overhead and make the convergence visible for smaller values of n. 
\newline

For the multiplication with generators, if we compare matrices of 128,1024 and 4096,we get:
$T(1024)\div T(128) \approx 7.06$,
$T(4096)\div T(1024) \approx 4.06$.
For $O(\alpha^2 M(n))$ we expected 9.14 and 4.4 respectively. The second ratio is close to 4.4 but the first shows more deviation.
As the matrix multiplication calls the vector multiplication internally, it suffers from the same overhead of copying values into polynomials $O(\alpha^2 n)$,which is not yet negligible compared to M(n) for small values of n.
The complexity is therefore $O(\alpha^2 M(n))$ asymptotically respected.
