\subsection{Addition}
\subsubsection{Algorithm}
With the displacement generators, we can simplify the addition of 2 matrices and thus reduce its cost by concatenating the generators of the 2 matrices.
From the property 10.7 and 10.8, we can pass from $O(n^2)$ for the naive algorithm to $O( \alpha \times n)$ with this algorithm.

 \begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Generator Addition}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Function{gr\_mat\_mul\_generator}{$T,U,G,H$}

    \State $G_C \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_Concat\_Horizontal}{T,G}$
    \State $H_C \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_Concat\_Horizontal}{U,H}$
    \State \textbf{return} $G_C, H_C$
\EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Multiplication}
\subsubsection{Multiplication Vector}
Perform the matrix multiplication with their displacement generators needs a function that multiplies a vector with displacement generators to avoid recomposing the matrices.
\begin{equation} \label{eq:SigmaLUV}
    C = \sum_{k=1}^{\alpha} L(g_k) \, U(h_k)\,V
\end{equation}
To do that, we takes the formula for the reconstruction and we transform our displacement generators into polynomials and make every operation into them.
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Multiplication of displacement generators with a vector}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Function{gr\_mat\_mul\_vector}{$G, H, V$}

    \State $m \gets nbRows(H)$
    \For{$j \gets 0$ \textbf{to} $nbCols(V)$}

        \For{$k \gets 0$ \textbf{to} $nbCols(G)$}
            \State $Pg \gets \sum_{r=0}^{n-1} G[r, k]  x^r$ 
            \State $Ph \gets \sum_{r=0}^{m-1} H[r, k]  x^r$ 
            \State $P_{h\_rev} \gets \text{Reverse}(P_h, m)$

\subsubsection{Multiplication matrices}
With displacement generators we also can simplify the multiplication of 2 matrices and thus reduce its cost by concatenating the generators of the 2 matrices.
- The displacement generators A=(T,U) and B=(G,H) 

- $W = Z \times A \times Z^t \times G$

- $V = B^t \times U$

- a (resp. b) a vector which is the last column of $ Z \times A $ (resp. $Z \times B^t$)

This allows us to pass from $O(n^3)$ for the naive algorithm to $O( \alpha^2 \times M(n) )$ for the version we implement.
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Generator Multiplication}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Function{gr\_mat\_mul\_generator}{$T,U,G,H$}

    \State $W_{Temp} \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_apply\_Zt}{G}$
    \State $W \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_mul\_vector}{T,U,W_{Temp}}$
    \State $W \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_apply\_Z}{W}$
    \newline
    \State $V \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_mul\_vector}{H,G,U}$
    \newline
    \State $a \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_mul\_vector}{T,U,LastCol}$
    \State $a \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_apply\_Z}{a}$
    \newline
    \State $b \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_mul\_vector}{H,G,LastCol}$
    \State $b \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_apply\_Z}{b}$
    \State $b \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_neg}{b}$
    \newline
    \State $G_C \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_concat\_horizontal}{T,W,a}$
    \State $H_C \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_concat\_horizontal}{V,H,b}$
    \State \textbf{return} $G_C, H_C$
\EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
We use the vector $LastCol = (0,.........,0,1)^T$, with the numbers of rows of the matrix you to retrieve the last column. This vector is composed with 0 except for the last element which is fixed to 1.
