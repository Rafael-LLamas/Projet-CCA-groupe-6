\section{Basic Operations}
\label{ch:operations}

Arithmetic operations on matrices are fundamental to algorithmic
efficiency. In this section, we see how to fully exploit the
properties of Toeplitz-like matrices to significantly reduce their
computational complexity bounds. First, we detail an optimized
approach to matrix addition. Next, we analyze the low computational
cost of matrix-vector multiplication. Finally, we conclude by
demonstrating how to perform full matrix multiplication directly
using displacement generators.

Throughout this section, we consider structured matrices $A$ and $B$,
represented by their displacement generators $(T, U)$ and $(G, H)$
respectively, in accordance with the properties defined previously.

\subsection{Addition}

\subsubsection{Generator Addition Algorithm}

Using displacement generators, the addition of two structured
matrices can be performed efficiently by simply concatenating their
respective generators. As established by the properties of
displacement operators (Properties 10.7 and 10.8 in
\cite{aecf-2017-pdf}), this approach reduces the computational
complexity of matrix addition from $O(n^2)$ for a naive
dense algorithm down to $O(\alpha n)$.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
  \caption{Generator Addition}

  \textbf{Input:} \\
  \hspace*{0.5cm} \textbullet \ $T, U$: Displacement generators of
  structured matrix $A \in \mathbb{K}^{n \times m}$ \\
  \hspace*{0.5cm} \textbullet \ $G, H$: Displacement generators of
  structured matrix $B \in \mathbb{K}^{n \times m}$ \\
  \textbf{Output:} $G_C, H_C$: Displacement generators representing
  the sum $A + B$

  \vspace{0.15cm}
  \hrule
  \vspace{0.15cm}

  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Function{gr\_mat\_add\_generators}{$T,U,G,H$}
    \State $G_C \gets [T \mid G]$ \Comment{Horizontal concatenation}
    \State $H_C \gets [U \mid H]$ \Comment{Horizontal concatenation}
    \State \textbf{return} $G_C, H_C$
    \EndFunction
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{Generator Addition Example}

To illustrate this, let $A$ and $B$ be two Toeplitz-like matrices
defined over the finite field $\mathbb{Z}/13\mathbb{Z}$, and let $C$
denote their naive sum ($C = A + B \pmod{13}$):

\begin{equation*}
  A =
  \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 7 & 10 \\ 5 & 0 & 7 \\ 0 & 5 & 0
  \end{pmatrix}, \quad
  B =
  \begin{pmatrix} 9 & 12 & 4 \\ 0 & 9 & 12 \\ 3 & 0 & 9
  \end{pmatrix}, \quad
  C =
  \begin{pmatrix} 9 & 6 & 1 \\ 5 & 9 & 6 \\ 3 & 5 & 9
  \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation*}

\noindent
Suppose the displacement generators for $A$ are given by $(T, U)$ and
for $B$ by $(G, H)$:

\begin{equation*}
  T =
  \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0
  \end{pmatrix}, \quad
  U =
  \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 0 \\ 0 & 7 \\ 0 & 10
  \end{pmatrix}, \quad
  G =
  \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \\ 9 & 1
  \end{pmatrix}, \quad
  H =
  \begin{pmatrix} 9 & 0 \\ 12 & 9 \\ 4 & 3
  \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation*}

\noindent
By applying the generator addition, we compute the
generators of the result, $G_C$ and $H_C$, through ordered horizontal
concatenation ($G_C = [T \mid G]$ and $H_C = [U \mid H]$):

\begin{equation*}
  G_C =
  \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 9 & 1
  \end{pmatrix}, \quad
  H_C =
  \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 0 & 9 & 0 \\ 0 & 7 & 12 & 9 \\ 0 & 10 & 4 & 3
  \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation*}

\noindent
Reconstructing the dense matrix $D$ directly from these concatenated
generators yields:

\begin{equation*}
  D =
  \begin{pmatrix} 9 & 6 & 1 \\ 5 & 9 & 6 \\ 3 & 5 & 9
  \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation*}

\noindent
The reconstructed matrix $D$ is equal to $C$, validating
the generator addition.

To verify the theoretical complexity of $\mathcal{O}(\alpha n)$, we
analyze the growth rate of the execution time $T(n)$ as the matrix
dimension $n$ increases.
Assuming $\alpha$ remains a small constant, a linear time complexity
dictates that multiplying the input size by a scalar $k$ should increase the
runtime by approximately the same factor $k$.

When scaling the matrix size from $n=128$ to $n=1024$ (an $8\times$
increase), the measured runtime ratio is:
$$ \frac{T(1024)}{T(128)} \approx 8.96 $$

Similarly, scaling from $n=1024$ to $n=4096$ (a $4\times$ increase)
yields a ratio of:
$$ \frac{T(4096)}{T(1024)} \approx 3.58 $$

These observed ratios align with the expected theoretical factors of
8 and 4. The slight deviations from perfect linear result can
be attributed to the low overall computational cost of the
operation, but overall these empirical results
assure the expected $O(\alpha n)$ time complexity.

\subsection{Multiplication}

\subsubsection{Matrix-Vector Generator Multiplication}

To perform full matrix multiplication efficiently using displacement
generators, we must first define a subroutine that multiplies a
structured matrix directly by a dense vector (or a block of vectors).
We aim to avoid the necessity of reconstructing the dense matrix from
its generators.
We define $V \in \mathbb{K}^{m \times p}$ where $p$ represents a
variable number of columns. We modify the reconstruction formula
\ref{eq:SigmaLU} to apply this multiplication.

\begin{equation} \label{eq:SigmaLUV}
  C = \sum_{k=1}^{\alpha} L(g_k) \, U(h_k) \, V
\end{equation}

We evaluate the reconstruction formula by mapping
the columns of the displacement generators and the vector into
polynomials which allows us to compute from the triangular Toeplitz
operators entirely through polynomial multiplication.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
  \caption{Multiplication of displacement generators with a vector/matrix}

  \textbf{Input:} \\
  \hspace*{0.5cm} \textbullet \ $G, H$: Displacement generators of
  structured matrix $A \in \mathbb{K}^{n \times m}$ \\
  \hspace*{0.5cm} \textbullet \ $V$: Dense vector or matrix $\in
  \mathbb{K}^{m \times p}$ \\
  \textbf{Output:} $C$: Resulting product matrix $\in \mathbb{K}^{n \times p}$

  \vspace{0.15cm}
  \hrule
  \vspace{0.15cm}

  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Function{gr\_mat\_mul\_vector}{$G, H, V$}
    \State $n \gets \text{nbRows}(G)$
    \State $m \gets \text{nbRows}(H)$
    \State Initialize $C$ as a zero matrix of size $n \times
    \text{nbCols}(V)$ \Comment{Initialize accumulator matrix}
    \For{$j \gets 0$ \textbf{to} $\text{nbCols}(V) - 1$}
    \State $P_v \gets \sum_{r=0}^{m-1} V[r, j] x^r$ \Comment{Map the
    $j$-th column of $V$ to a polynomial}
    \For{$k \gets 0$ \textbf{to} $\text{nbCols}(G) - 1$}
    \State $P_g \gets \sum_{r=0}^{n-1} G[r, k] x^r$ \Comment{Map the
    $k$-th column of generator $G$}
    \State $P_h \gets \sum_{r=0}^{m-1} H[r, k] x^r$ \Comment{Map the
    $k$-th column of generator $H$}

    \State $P_{h\_rev} \gets \text{Reverse}(P_h, m)$ \Comment{Reverse
    $H$ to simulate upper Toeplitz operator}
    \State $P_{tmp} \gets P_{h\_rev} P_v$ \Comment{Apply upper
    Toeplitz structure to the vector}
    \State $P_{tmp\_shift} \gets P_{tmp} \gg (m - 1)$ \Comment{Shift
    to extract valid convolution coefficients}
    \State $P_{res} \gets P_g P_{tmp\_shift}$ \Comment{Apply lower
    Toeplitz structure}

    \For{$i \gets 0$ \textbf{to} $\text{Length}(P_{res}) - 1$}
    \State $C[i, j] \gets C[i, j] + \text{Coefficient}(P_{res}, i)$
    \Comment{Accumulate results into $C$}
    \EndFor
    \EndFor
    \EndFor
    \State \textbf{return} $C$
    \EndFunction
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{Matrix-Matrix Generator Multiplication}
\label{section:matrixmatrixmul}

Using displacement generators, the full multiplication of two
structured matrices can be computed by extracting the
boundary shifts and concatenating the resulting generators.
For matrices $A \approx (T,U)$ and $B \approx (G,H)$, this requires computing
specific intermediate sub-blocks $W = Z A Z^T G$ and $V = B^T U$, as
well as extracting
the final boundary columns $a$ and $b$ by multiplying these sub
blocks with the basis vector of $e^{(m)}_{m-1} = (0, ... 0, 1)^T $.

By performing these operations entirely in the compressed generator
space, we reduce the computational complexity from the naive
$O(n^3)$ bound down to $O(\alpha^2 M(n))$, where $M(n)$ represents the
time required to multiply two polynomials of degree $n$.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
  \caption{Generator Multiplication}

  \textbf{Input:} \\
  \hspace*{0.5cm} \textbullet \ $T, U$: Displacement generators of
  structured matrix $A \in \mathbb{K}^{n \times m}$ \\
  \hspace*{0.5cm} \textbullet \ $G, H$: Displacement generators of
  structured matrix $B \in \mathbb{K}^{m \times k}$ \\
  \textbf{Output:} $G_C, H_C$: Displacement generators representing
  the product $A \times B$

  \vspace{0.15cm}
  \hrule
  \vspace{0.15cm}

  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Function{gr\_mat\_mul\_generators}{$T,U,G,H$}
    \State $e^{(m)}_{m-1} \gets (0, \dots, 0, 1)^T$ \Comment{Basis vector
    to extract the last column}
    \vspace{0.1cm}

    \State $W_{Temp} \gets Z^T \times G$
    \State $W \gets Z \times \Call{gr\_mat\_mul\_vector}{T, U, W_{Temp}}$
    \Comment{Vector multiplication of FLINT\cite{flint331}}
    \vspace{0.1cm}

    \State $V \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_mul\_vector}{H, G, U}$
    \vspace{0.1cm}

    \State $a \gets Z \times \Call{gr\_mat\_mul\_vector}{T, U, e_{n-1}}$
    \State $b \gets -Z \times \Call{gr\_mat\_mul\_vector}{H, G, e_{n-1}}$
    \vspace{0.1cm}

    \State $G_C \gets [T \mid W \mid a]$ \Comment{Horizontal concatenation}
    \State $H_C \gets [V \mid H \mid b]$ \Comment{Horizontal concatenation}
    \State \textbf{return} $G_C, H_C$
    \EndFunction
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsubsection{Generator Multiplication Example}
We takes A and B, two randoms matrices, C the result of A and B.
Every element is in $\mathbb{Z}/_{13}\mathbb{Z}$.

\begin{equation}
  A =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    10    & 5     & 8 \\
    5     & 10     & 5 \\
    3     & 5     & 10
  \end{pmatrix},
  B =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    0     & 3     & 0 \\
    3    & 0     & 3 \\
    0     &3     & 0
  \end{pmatrix},
  C =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    2     & 2     & 2 \\
    4     & 4     & 4 \\
    2     & 0     & 2
  \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}

We take displacement generators:

\begin{equation}
  T =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1    & 0     \\
    7     & 1    \\
    12     & 11
  \end{pmatrix},
  U =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    10     & 0      \\
    5     & 4      \\
    8     & 9
  \end{pmatrix},
  G =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    0    & 1      \\
    1     & 0      \\
    0     & 0
  \end{pmatrix},
  H =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    3     & 0      \\
    0     & 3      \\
    0     & 0
  \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}

We need to calculate W and V before concatenating the generators of A
and B to obtain the displacement generator of D.

\begin{equation}
  W =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    0    & 0 \\
    10     & 0 \\
    5     & 0
  \end{pmatrix},
  V =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    2     & 12    \\
    2    & 1      \\
    2     & 12
  \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}

Then we do the concatenation of T,W,a and V,H,-b respectively to obtain $RES_G$
and $RES_H$.

\begin{equation}
  RES_G =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    1     & 0     & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
    7    & 1     & 10 & 0 & 8 \\
    12     & 11     & 5  & 0 & 5
  \end{pmatrix},
  RES_H =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    2     & 12     & 3 & 0 & 0 \\
    2    & 1     & 0 & 3 & 0 \\
    2     & 12     & 0  & 0 & 10
  \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}

The resulting matrix D :

\begin{equation}
  D =
  \begin{pmatrix}
    2     & 2     & 2 \\
    4     & 4     & 4 \\
    2     & 0     & 2
  \end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}

We can see that the matrix D is equal to C, our way to multiply the 2
matrices is correct.

\subsection{The Challenge of Rank Growth}

While the arithmetic operations described above achieve
efficient complexities, chaining these
operations introduces a severe vulnerability. As
demonstrated in the generator addition and multiplication algorithms,
the output generators are formed via horizontal concatenation.
Consequently, performing arithmetic on two matrices with a
displacement rank of $\alpha$ inflates the resulting
matrix's displacement rank (ex. addition results in a rank of $2\alpha$).

If multiple operations are performed sequentially such as in
divide-and-conquer matrix inversion or iterative system solvers, this
rank will grow exponentially. To maintain our
efficiency across complex computations, we must actively reduce the
dimensions of these intermediate generators without changing the
source dense matrix they represent.
