\section{Introduction}

In computational numerical linear algebra, 
dense matrices often present a computational bottleneck, 
scaling quadratically in terms of memory usage ($O(nm)$) on a matrix with $n$ rows and $m$ columns
and cubically in terms of time complexity ($O(n^3)$) for standard operations. 
However, these structured matrices are utilised by a wide class of systems
such as signal processing\cite{7400965}\cite{signals5030021} applications and classical quantum simulation\cite{10.1098/rsos.160906}.
In these systems, the elements are arranged in repeating, deterministic patterns. 
Designing algorithms that take advantage of this pattern can bypass the fundamental 
limitations of dense linear algebra.
