\section{Generator Compression}
\label{ch:compression}

Matrices $G \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times \alpha}$ and $H \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times \alpha}$ representing a displacement matrix $\nabla A = G H^T$, where the actual rank of $\nabla A$ is $r < \alpha$.
\vspace{0.5em}

\textbf{Goal:} Find generators $G' \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times r}$ and $H' \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times r}$ s.t. the product is the same:
\[
    G'H'^T = G H^T
\]

We will achieve this by independently compression both matrices and applying the inverse of the operations done to the other.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Generator Compression}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Function{gr\_mat\_generator\_compress}{$G_A, H_A$}
    \State $G_1, H_1 \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_generator\_compress\_aux}{G_A, H_A}$
    \State $H_D, G_D \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_generator\_compress\_aux}{H_1, G_1}$
    \State \textbf{return} $G_D, H_D$
\EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Generator Compression Auxiliary}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Function{gr\_mat\_generator\_compress\_aux}{$G, H$}
    \State $P, L, U \gets \text{LU\_Decomposition}(G^T)$ \Comment{$P G^T = L U$}
    \State $r \gets \text{Number of non-zero rows in } U$ \Comment{rank}
    \If{$r < 1$}
        \State \textbf{return} Generators of a $0$ matrix
    \EndIf
    \State $G_{D} \gets (U_{[0:r-1],*})^T$
    \State $H_{D} \gets H P^T \times L_{*,[0:r-1]}$
    \State \textbf{return} $G_D, H_D$
\EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Note that calling \textsc{gr\_mat\_generator\_compress\_aux} twice with swapped arguments (and variables) is valid because each call preserves the product $GH^T$, 
as can be verified from the LU factorization.

\begin{proof}
We show that the second call to the auxiliary function preserves the generator product, meaning $H_D G_D^T = H_1 G_1^T$. 

First argument is $H_1$, the LU decomposition is performed on $H_1^T$ giving $P H_1^T = L U$. 
Since $U$ has rank $r$, the product only depends on the first $r$ columns and rows: $LU = L_{*,[0:r-1]}\, U_{[0:r-1],*}$.
Starting from $P H_1^T = LU$ and multiplying both sides on the left by $P^T$:
\begin{align*}
    H_1^T &= P^T L_{*,[0:r-1]}\, U_{[0:r-1],*} \\
    H_1 &= \bigl(U_{[0:r-1],*}\bigr)^T L_{*,[0:r-1]}^T\, P
\end{align*}
Based on the swapped inputs to the auxiliary function, returned generators are given as $H_D = \bigl(U_{[0:r-1],*}\bigr)^T$ and $G_D = G_1 P^T L_{*,[0:r-1]}$. 
Taking the transpose of $G_D$:
\[
    G_D^T = \bigl(G_1 P^T L_{*,[0:r-1]}\bigr)^T = L_{*,[0:r-1]}^T\, P\, G_1^T
\]
For $H_D G_D^T$, we see $H_1$ form:
\begin{align*}
    H_D G_D^T &= \bigl(U_{[0:r-1],*}\bigr)^T \Bigl( L_{*,[0:r-1]}^T\, P\, G_1^T \Bigr) \\
              &= \Bigl( \bigl(U_{[0:r-1],*}\bigr)^T L_{*,[0:r-1]}^T\, P \Bigr) G_1^T \\
              &= H_1 G_1^T.
\end{align*}
Since $H_D G_D^T = H_1 G_1^T$, transposing gives us $G_D H_D^T = G_1 H_1^T$.
\end{proof}

Computing the LU factorization of the transposed generator $G^T \in \mathbb{K}^{\alpha \times n}$ requires $\mathcal{O}(\alpha^2 n)$ operations. 
Extracting $U$ and applying the permutation matrix $P^T$ to $H$ is in $O(\alpha n)$\footnote{In FLINT\cite{flint331}, the permutation matrix is represented as an array of indices. We do a swapping operation rather than a full matrix multiplication.}.
Computing $H_{D} = H P^T \times L_{*,[0:r-1]}$ contains a multiplication of a $n \times \alpha$ matrix by a $\alpha \times r$ matrix. Using standard matrix multiplication, this takes $O(n \alpha r)$ operations. 
Our implementation uses loops to extract the selected amount of rows and columns, but their complexities are strictly bounded by $O(n \alpha r)$.

We can generalize this complexity: for any generator matrices in $\mathbb{K}^{n \times m}$ compressing to rank $r$, the overall time complexity for the generator compression is dominated by the matrix multiplication and is bounded by $O(r n m)$.
