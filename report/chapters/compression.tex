\section{Generator Compression}
\label{ch:compression}

Matrices $G \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times \alpha}$ and $H \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times \alpha}$ representing a displacement matrix $\nabla A = G H^T$, where the actual rank of $\nabla A$ is $r < \alpha$.
\vspace{0.5em}

\textbf{Goal:} Find generators $G' \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times r}$ and $H' \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times r}$ s.t. the product is the same:
\[
    G'H'^T = G H^T
\]

We will achieve this by independently compression both matrices and applying the inverse of the operations done to the other.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Generator Compression}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Function{gr\_mat\_generator\_compress}{$G_A, H_A$}
    \State $G_1, H_1 \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_generator\_compress\_aux}{G_A, H_A}$
    \State $H_D, G_D \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_generator\_compress\_aux}{H_1, G_1}$
    \State \textbf{return} $G_D, H_D$
\EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Generator Compression Auxiliary}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Function{gr\_mat\_generator\_compress\_aux}{$G, H$}
    \State $P, L, U \gets \text{LU\_Decomposition}(G^T)$ \Comment{$P G^T = L U$}
    \State $r \gets \text{Number of non-zero rows in } U$ \Comment{rank}
    \If{$r < 1$}
        \State \textbf{return} Generators of a $0$ matrix
    \EndIf
    \State $G_{D} \gets (U_{[0:r-1],*})^T$
    \State $H_{D} \gets H P^T \times L_{*,[0:r-1]}$
    \State \textbf{return} $G_D, H_D$
\EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Note that calling \textsc{gr\_mat\_generator\_compress\_aux} twice with swapped arguments (and variables) is valid because each call preserves the product $GH^T$, 
as can be verified from the LU factorization.

\begin{proof}
We show that $G_D H_D^T = G H^T$, that the auxiliary function 
preserves the generator product.
Since $U$ has rank $r$, its last $k - r$ rows are zero by LU decomposition. 
Therefore the product $LU$ only depends on the first $r$ columns of $L$ and rows of $U$: $LU = L_{*,[0:r-1]}\, U_{[0:r-1],*}$.

\vspace{0.1cm}

Starting from $PG^T = LU$ and multiplying both sides on the left by $P^T$:
\[
    G^T = P^T L_{*,[0:r-1]}\, U_{[0:r-1],*} \quad \text{($P^T \times P = I$ since $P$ is a permutation matrix)}
\]
\[
    G = \bigl(U_{[0:r-1],*}\bigr)^T L_{*,[0:r-1]}^T\, P.
\]
We now have the definitions $G_D = \bigl(U_{[0:r-1],*}\bigr)^T$ 
and $H_D = H P^T L_{*,[0:r-1]}$ and expand $G_D H_D^T$:
\begin{align*}
    G_D H_D^T 
    &= \bigl(U_{[0:r-1],*}\bigr)^T \bigl(H P^T L_{*,[0:r-1]}\bigr)^T \\
    &= \bigl(U_{[0:r-1],*}\bigr)^T L_{*,[0:r-1]}^T\, P\, H^T \\
    &= G H^T.
\end{align*}
\end{proof}

Computing the LU factorization of the transposed generator $G^T \in \mathbb{K}^{r \times n}$ requires $\mathcal{O}(r^2 n)$ operations.
Extracting $U$ and applying the permutation matrix $P^T$ to $H$ is in $\mathcal{O}(r n)$.
Computing the new right factor $H_{D} = H P^T \times L_{*,[0:r-1]}$ involves multiplying an $n \times k$ matrix by a $k \times r$ matrix. Using standard matrix multiplication, this takes $\mathcal{O}(r^2 n)$ operations.
Our implementation is using loops to get the selected amount of rows and columns but their complexities are bounded by $\mathcal{O}(r^2 n)$.

We can generalize the complexity by revising for any matrix in $\mathbb{K}^{m \times n}$ the overall time complexity for the generator compression is $\mathcal{O}(r m n)$.

Our implementation can be found in \ref{annexe:compression}.
