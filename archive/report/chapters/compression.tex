\section{Generator Compression}
\label{ch:compression}

As shown at the end of section \ref{ch:operations},
operations cause the displacement rank $\alpha$ to grow rapidly.
To maintain efficiency, we must compress the generator
matrices.

Let $G \in \mathbb{K}^{n \times \alpha}$ and $H \in \mathbb{K}^{n
\times \alpha}$ be displacement generators representing a
displacement matrix $\nabla A = G H^T$. The true mathematical rank of
the displacement matrix is $r = \text{rank}(\nabla A) < \alpha$.

\vspace{0.5em}
\noindent\textbf{Goal:} Compute new, compressed generators $G' \in
\mathbb{K}^{n \times r}$ and $H' \in \mathbb{K}^{n \times r}$, where
$r$ is minimal and:
\[
  G'H'^T = G H^T
\]

We achieve this reduction by sequentially compressing each matrix
using an LU factorization and applying the inverse operations to the
other matrix to preserve the product.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
  \caption{Generator Compression} \label{alg:generator_compression}

  \textbf{Input:} \\
  \hspace*{0.5cm} \textbullet \ $G_A, H_A$: Uncompressed generators
  $\in \mathbb{K}^{n \times \alpha}$ \\
  \textbf{Output:} $G_D, H_D$: Compressed generators $\in
  \mathbb{K}^{n \times r}$ where $r \le \alpha$

  \vspace{0.15cm}
  \hrule
  \vspace{0.15cm}

  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Function{gr\_mat\_generator\_compress}{$G_A, H_A$}
    \State $G_1, H_1 \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_generator\_compress\_aux}{G_A, H_A}$
    \State $H_D, G_D \gets \Call{gr\_mat\_generator\_compress\_aux}{H_1, G_1}$
    \State \textbf{return} $G_D, H_D$
    \EndFunction
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
  \caption{Generator Compression Auxiliary}

  \textbf{Input:} \\
  \hspace*{0.5cm} \textbullet \ $G, H$: Generators to be partially compressed \\
  \textbf{Output:} $G_D, H_D$: Partially compressed generators

  \vspace{0.15cm}
  \hrule
  \vspace{0.15cm}

  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Function{gr\_mat\_generator\_compress\_aux}{$G, H$}
    \State $P, L, U \gets \text{LU\_Decomposition}(G^T)$
    \Comment{$P G^T = L U$}
    \State $r \gets \text{Number of non-zero rows in } U$
    \Comment{Extracts the true rank $r$}
    \If{$r < 1$}
    \State \textbf{return} Generators of a $0$ matrix
    \EndIf
    \State $G_{D} \gets (U_{[0:r-1],*})^T$
    \State $H_{D} \gets H P^T L_{*,[0:r-1]}$
    \State \textbf{return} $G_D, H_D$
    \EndFunction
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Note that calling the auxiliary function twice with swapped arguments
is valid because each individual call inherently preserves the matrix
product.

\begin{proposition}
  Algorithm \ref{alg:generator_compression} is correct. Specifically,
  the auxiliary function preserves the generator product,
  guaranteeing that $H_D G_D^T = H_1 G_1^T$, which consequently
  ensures $G_D H_D^T = G_A H_A^T$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
  We show that the second call to the auxiliary function preserves
  the generator product. In this step, the first argument is $H_1$,
  meaning the LU decomposition is performed on $H_1^T$, resulting $P
  H_1^T = L U$.

  Since $U$ has a rank of $r$, the matrix product depends exclusively
  on the first $r$ columns and rows:
  \[ LU = L_{*,[0:r-1]}\, U_{[0:r-1],*} \]

  Starting from $P H_1^T = LU$ and multiplying both sides on the left
  by $P^T$, we obtain:
  \begin{align*}
    H_1^T &= P^T L_{*,[0:r-1]}\, U_{[0:r-1],*} \\
    H_1 &= \bigl(U_{[0:r-1],*}\bigr)^T L_{*,[0:r-1]}^T\, P
  \end{align*}

  Based on the logic of the auxiliary function, the returned
  generators are $H_D = \bigl(U_{[0:r-1],*}\bigr)^T$ and $G_D = G_1
  P^T L_{*,[0:r-1]}$. Taking the transpose of $G_D$ gives:
  \[
    G_D^T = \bigl(G_1 P^T L_{*,[0:r-1]}\bigr)^T = L_{*,[0:r-1]}^T\, P\, G_1^T
  \]

  Substituting these into the product $H_D G_D^T$, we reconstruct the
  original form of $H_1$:
  \begin{align*}
    H_D G_D^T &= \bigl(U_{[0:r-1],*}\bigr)^T \Bigl( L_{*,[0:r-1]}^T\,
    P\, G_1^T \Bigr) \\
    &= \Bigl( \bigl(U_{[0:r-1],*}\bigr)^T L_{*,[0:r-1]}^T\, P \Bigr) G_1^T \\
    &= H_1 G_1^T
  \end{align*}

  Since $H_D G_D^T = H_1 G_1^T$, taking the transpose of both sides
  gives us $G_D H_D^T = G_1 H_1^T$.
\end{proof}

Computing the LU factorization of the transposed generator $G^T \in
\mathbb{K}^{\alpha \times n}$ requires $O(\alpha^2 n)$ operations.
Extracting $U$ and applying the permutation matrix $P^T$ to $H$ is in
$O(\alpha n)$\footnote{In FLINT\cite{flint331}, the permutation
  matrix is represented as an array of indices. We do a swapping
operation rather than a full matrix multiplication.}.
Computing $H_{D} = H P^T \times L_{*,[0:r-1]}$ contains a
multiplication of a $n \times \alpha$ matrix by a $\alpha \times r$
matrix. Using standard matrix multiplication, this takes $O(n \alpha
r)$ operations.
Our implementation uses loops to extract the selected amount of rows
and columns, but their complexities are strictly bounded by $O(n \alpha r)$.

We can generalize this complexity: for any generator matrices in
$\mathbb{K}^{n \times m}$ compressing to rank $r$, the overall time
complexity for the generator compression is dominated by the matrix
multiplication and is bounded by $O(r n m)$.
